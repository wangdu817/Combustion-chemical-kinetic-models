## Abstract

The contamination of ammonium nitrate (AN) with chlorides is known to significantly decrease the thermal stability of this compound. Although the thermal characteristics and reaction kinetics of AN/chloride mixtures have been thoroughly investigated, the mechanism associated with the condensed phase pyrolysis of such mixtures remains unclear. The present study employed quantum chemistry calculations at the G4//ωB97X-D/SMD level of theory to analyze the condensed phase pyrolysis of AN/ammonium chloride (NH4Cl) mixtures. A detailed kinetic model incorporating 72 chemical species and 134 elementary reactions and based on first principles was constructed for this purpose. Using this model, reaction simulations were performed to predict the heat flow profile and variations in reactants and products during AN/NH4Cl pyrolysis. The heat flow profile was then compared with experimental data obtained using differential scanning calorimetry. The predicted reaction onset temperature, heat release rate and total heat of pyrolysis were in good agreement with the experimental results. The pyrolysis pathways for this system were determined and HCl was found to act as a catalyst. This process is evidently initiated by the reaction of HCl with HNO3 to form NO2Cl, leading to the formation and subsequent decomposition of NO2ClNH3 to generate HONO and NH2Cl. This NH2Cl decomposes to produce trans-N2H2 and regenerate HCl, establishing the catalytic cycle AN → 0.5 trans-N2H2 + HONO + H2O. The HONO further accelerates AN decomposition as AN + HONO → N2 + 2H2O + HNO3.
