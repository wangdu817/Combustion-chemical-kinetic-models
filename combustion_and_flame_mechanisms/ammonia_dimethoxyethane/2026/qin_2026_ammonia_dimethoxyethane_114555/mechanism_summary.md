## Abstract

Ammonia (NH3) is a promising carbon-free green fuel, but faces challenges such as low reactivity and difficulty in ignition. Adding active additives to enhance the combustion reactivity of NH3 is an ideal solution and has practical significance for improving the combustion performance of NH3. In this work, the high-temperature co-oxidation of 1,2-dimethoxyethane (1,2-DME) and NH3 in a jet-stirred reactor was conducted under atmospheric pressure and within a temperature range of 740–1070 K. The reactants, products, and intermediates were measured and identified using synchrotron vacuum ultraviolet photoionization mass spectrometry (SVUV-PIMS), and their mole fraction distributions were finally quantified. A series of key products were determined during the 1,2-DME/NH3 co-oxidation, especially nitrogen oxides and carbon–nitrogen intermediates such as NO, NO2, N2O, CH2NH, HCNO, HONO, CH3NO2, and HCN. These key intermediates are crucial for clarifying the reaction pathways of 1,2-DME/NH3 co-oxidation. Based on the experimental measurements and theoretical calculations, a detailed high-temperature co-oxidation model of NH3/1,2-DME was developed by coupling the 1,2-DME mechanism, NH3 mechanism, and C–N interaction pathways. The model demonstrates good predictive capability for the species distributions obtained in this work. Sensitivity and reaction pathway analyses revealed that NH3 consumption below 850 K is primarily controlled by 1,2-DME sub-chemistry, such as the C–C bond dissociation of 1,2-DME-derived radicals; at 850–1000 K, NH3 consumption is further influenced by the OH radical formation pathways involving C0–C1 core species; at temperatures above 1000 K, NH3 consumption is dominated by the spontaneous oxidation of NH3 and the reaction pathways of O2 + H = O + OH and H + O2 (+M) = HO2 (+M).
